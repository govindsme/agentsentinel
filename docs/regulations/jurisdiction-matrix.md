# Jurisdiction Matrix

AI regulation differs materially by jurisdiction. This page consolidates the current
landscape; the machine-readable data is in `data/jurisdiction-matrix.json`.

!!! warning "Revalidate regularly"
    Move fast in this area. Confirm status and applicability on every reconciliation run.

## Global view

| Jurisdiction | Maturity | Primary instruments | Enforceability |
|--------------|----------|---------------------|----------------|
| **European Union** | High | EU AI Act; GDPR; AI Liability (proposed) | Binding |
| **United Kingdom** | Medium | Sector-led approach; proposed AI legislation; UK GDPR | Sector-based |
| **United States** | Medium | EO on Safe/Secure/Trustworthy AI; NIST AI RMF (voluntary); sector rules | Sector-based |
| **Canada** | Medium | AIDA (proposed); federal Directive on Automated Decision-Making | Proposed |
| **China** | High | Generative AI Measures; Recommendation Algorithm Regs; Deep Synthesis Provisions | Binding |
| **Singapore** | Medium | Model AI Governance Framework; Generative AI Framework | Voluntary |

## Key themes

- **Risk-based tiering** — the EU AI Act and other schemes classify AI by risk level.
- **Sector-level regulation** — health, finance, and public-procurement often have AI rules first.
- **Voluntary frameworks** — NIST AI RMF and Singapore frameworks influence practice globally.
- **Data protection overlap** — GDPR/UK GDPR and regional equivalents apply to AI processing personal data.

## Organizational implications

- **Map your footprint** — determine which jurisdictions your AI systems and data touch.
- **Classify each system** — apply the strictest applicable requirements for each jurisdiction.
- **Monitor change** — track emerging instruments and adjust the compliance program.
- **Maintain a register** — keep a living record of obligations per system and market.
